## Role 3: Cinematographer

```
ROLE: Cinematographer
VERDICT: CONDITIONAL PASS
SCORE: 7/10
```

**BLOCKING ISSUES:**

1. **Shot 14 final hold is 1.5 seconds — critically short for the chapter's intended climax.** The Director Brief (Section 7) specifies "completely still for the last five to eight seconds." The shot list's own note acknowledges the 1.5s is compressed and states "extend if edit allows." At 1.5s this shot cannot do what the Director requires: the viewer cannot land, breathe, and carry the unanswered question forward in ninety frames. The moraine dam sequence (Shots 13–14 combined) totals 2.5 seconds. This is the most important sequence in the chapter and it gets less screen time than Shot 03 (the grey glacier flyover). This is not a note — it is a structural problem that undermines Beat 3 entirely. The shot list must either reclaim time from Beat 2 or formally lower its ambition for the closing beat. Fix: redistribute 1.5s from Beat 2 (Shot 10 overlay is 2.5s; it could lose 1.0s without harm) to extend Shot 14 to a minimum of 3.0s.

2. **Shot 08 / Shot 09 moraine-dam-hide geometry assumption is unverified.** The shot list correctly states the moraine dam must be "tall enough to screen the lake until the camera clears it — approximately 40–50 m above valley floor." However, the technical spec (Section 2, Shot 08) notes the camera end-altitude is 5,810 m ASL and the lake surface is 5,010 m ASL — an 800 m AGL camera looking east-northeast toward a 40–50 m dam ridge. At this camera height and angle, the 40–50 m dam crest geometry does NOT reliably screen an 800 m AGL camera's sightline to the full 1.56 km² lake behind it. The geometry is angular-incidence dependent: the lake's western end (closest to the camera) sits directly behind the moraine dam, but the lake's eastern two-thirds are visible from 800 m altitude at most reasonable camera angles. The reveal relies on the lake being fully hidden in Shot 08, then fully revealed in Shot 09. If the eastern lake shore is visible in the final second of Shot 08, the reveal is broken. The TD must render a test frame at f572 and verify the lake is not visible. If it is, the end-altitude for Shot 08 must be raised, or the camera trajectory must be adjusted to approach from higher and steeper. This is a blocking geometry validation requirement.

3. **Shot 05 camera position (inside the Annapurna Sanctuary at 5,100 m absolute, looking up) is cinematographically inconsistent with the shot list's stated type ("Aerial wide").** The camera is described as "an aerial position inside the Sanctuary bowl" at 1,000 m AGL above the basin floor. But the camera is at 5,100 m absolute, which means it is sitting on the Sanctuary floor level, not above it — "1 km above the Sanctuary floor" is contradicted by the geographic position being at the floor's own elevation. The Research Brief establishes the Sanctuary floor at ~4,100 m; the camera should be at 5,100 m absolute (~1,000 m above floor). The technical spec confirms camera Blender location z=5.1 BU = 5,100 m ASL. This is internally consistent, but the 400mm telephoto compression from inside the bowl at 5,100 m looking up at peaks topping 8,091 m will produce severe vertical foreshortening: the camera is only 3,000 m below Annapurna I's summit, which at 400mm will compress rings of peaks at very different distances into a wall. The shot list calls this the intent. However, this is a narrow window: if the 400mm focal length + inside-bowl camera produces z-fighting (acknowledged in both the shot list and technical spec) and the fallback is orthographic projection, the telephoto compression — which is doing all the narrative work — is lost. The spec must commit to a specific mitigation for z-fighting before render rather than leaving it as a conditional note.

**NON-BLOCKING NOTES:**

1. **Visual variety across 14 shots is genuine and well-constructed.** The chapter moves from orthographic satellite (Shot 01) through 80 km perspective (Shot 02), 30–15 km descent (Shot 03), three 2 km regional statics (Shots 04–06), 500 m telephoto (Shot 07), a descending approach (Shot 08), two near-nadir lake statics (Shots 09–10), a low near-horizontal lateral track (Shot 11), a static close insert (Shot 12), and two progressive close-in statics of the moraine dam (Shots 13–14). The progression from orbital to 20 m above water is a genuine spatial story. The variety does not feel forced.

2. **The glory moment (Shot 01, 7s static) is set up to be visually overwhelming, not merely competent.** The color palette specification is precise: #D4622A summit alpenglow against #0D1B3E sky, with #0A0A0A plains below. The instruction to use the Nishita sky at 200,000 m altitude is correct — this will produce the deep cobalt-to-black gradient the Research Brief documents as "Himalayan blue." The single Cinematographer risk is the transition from stillness to Shot 02's eastward drift: the shot list describes this as "the mountain deciding to move rather than the camera deciding to pan." At 7 seconds of absolute static followed by a hard cut, the visual momentum depends entirely on the Karakoram being immediately dynamic on frame f211. If the Karakoram is similarly static-feeling, the cut dissipates the tension. This is a note, not a block.

3. **The three 1-second regional identification shots (Shots 04–06) are visually distinct by design.** Shot 04 is distinguishable by the debris-free blue-grey ice and proglacial bare rock. Shot 05 is distinguishable by the vertiginous enclosure and near-dark basin floor. Shot 06 is distinguishable by the bronze autumn forest foreground — the only shot in the chapter showing deciduous vegetation. These three shots will not blur together in the viewer's memory because each has a unique dominant color key: Shot 04 = blue-grey ice; Shot 05 = dark basin enclosure with orange summit rim; Shot 06 = bronze-to-white transition. The design is correct.

4. **The calving event (Shot 12) is correctly treated as static camera, small block, no score.** The specification (block volume ≤ 2.5 m³, wave amplitude ≤ 15 cm, camera absolutely static) matches the Director and Research Brief requirements. The Cinematographer note ("It is Tuesday") is the right framing for execution.

5. **Shot 11's camera height (20 m above lake surface) is the strongest compositional choice in the chapter.** Placing the viewer nearly at water level for the calving front lateral track establishes physical proximity to a large cold opaque body of water and unstable ice — an intimacy that is more unsettling than any aerial view. This is the cinematographic decision that makes the calving event in Shot 12 feel personally threatening rather than abstractly large-scale.

6. **The arc-to-Nepal transition (Shot 02 to Shot 03) works cinematographically because the altitude drop is built into the camera move of Shot 03 itself.** The cut from Shot 02 (80 km altitude, white Karakoram) to Shot 03 (30 km descending to 15 km, grey Nepal debris glaciers) produces the visual disruption the Director requires. The altitude change and the color change happen simultaneously on the cut — this is correct.

7. **The focal lengths and altitudes across 14 shots are cinematographically coherent and internally consistent.** The orthographic rig for Shots 01–03 and the perspective rig for Shots 04–14 are correctly separated. The focal length progression (300mm telephoto for wall-of-peaks effects → 50mm normal for true-scale lake reveal → 135mm slight telephoto for calving isolation → 35mm wide for dam-orientation → 85mm normal-to-medium for final hold) tells a spatial story through optics that reinforces the narrative arc.

## Role 4: Writer

```
ROLE: Writer
VERDICT: CONDITIONAL PASS
SCORE: 8/10
```

**BLOCKING ISSUES:**

1. **The Shot 09 "Imja Tsho" label appears at f630 (20.97s into the chapter) but the script's silence-discipline section incorrectly states "The first non-optional text element in the chapter is 'Imja Tsho' — a name, at f630, after 20.97 seconds of moving image."** This framing is accurate IF the three optional regional labels (Shots 04–06) are omitted. But Shot 09's name label is preceded by Shot 10's data overlay, which is at f646 — only 0.53 seconds after the "Imja Tsho" label disappears. This creates a 0-second gap between the name label fading out and the data overlay fading in: the "Imja Tsho" label cuts at f645 (it has fade-out: 0 frames — "cut with shot"), and Shot 10's 1962 overlay begins fading in at f646 with a 14-frame fade. This is technically compliant with the "numbers appear alone" text policy. However, the viewer who encounters "Imja Tsho" at f630→f645 and then immediately "1962 / ~0.03 km²" at f646 is being asked to absorb two distinct text events with zero visual breathing room. The Director Brief's pacing note specifies "at least four seconds before the next data element appears." The gap between the "Imja Tsho" label (f630–f645) and the 1962 overlay (f646) is one frame — a 0.03s gap, not four seconds. This violates the stated pacing rule. Fix: either push the "Imja Tsho" label earlier (f600–f620, while the viewer has already processed the color), or push the 1962 overlay to f690 and shorten Shot 10 accordingly.

2. **The "~0.03 km² — a cluster of meltwater pools" framing for Shot 10 is the right decision, but the dash construction is ambiguous.** The script presents it as: `"~0.03 km²  —  a cluster of meltwater pools"` as a single element. This reads as if the area measurement and the description are equivalent. For a viewer who parses quickly, "~0.03 km²" sits on screen simultaneously with "a cluster of meltwater pools" as a compound label, which is correct. But the em-dash construction `0.03 km²  —  a cluster of meltwater pools` could read as the description being a consequence or parenthetical of the measurement, rather than a clarification of what 0.03 km² represents. Suggested fix: break into two typographic lines — "~0.03 km²" on the first line, "a cluster of meltwater pools" on the second — using the xs weight with 2–3pt additional line-height, so the viewer reads description first, then connects it to the number. This does not change content; it changes reading order for non-fluent readers.

**NON-BLOCKING NOTES:**

1. **The 79–87% text-free ratio is appropriate.** At 79% text-free, the chapter retains its cinematic identity while accommodating the two essential data events (lake name, lake growth). At 87% (with optional labels omitted), it approaches the Baraka/Samsara register the Director references. The ratio is defensible and the justifications in the silence-discipline section are rigorous.

2. **"Imja Tsho" at f630 is earned, not premature.** By f630 the viewer has watched 20.97 seconds of imagery: the HKH arc, three regional fingerprints, the Khumbu Icefall (a recognition moment), the grey descent, and 44 frames of the milky turquoise lake in full. The name arrives after the viewer has been given enough visual context that the label functions as confirmation rather than instruction. This is correct pacing for a label.

3. **The regional identifier shots (Shots 04–06) with optional/conditional labels are correctly handled.** The Writer makes the right call: these labels are cartographic wayfinding, not narrative elements, and the "optional" flag is the honest answer to whether they are needed. A viewer who does not need wayfinding loses nothing from their absence; a viewer who does need wayfinding gets a minimal 9pt label that names but does not explain. The distinction between "LANGTANG" (the label) and "Langtang Valley, site of Yala Glacier, a benchmark monitoring site" (which the script explicitly rejects) is the correct editorial discipline.

4. **The script rigorously avoids emotionally manipulative language.** "Rapidly," "alarming," "massive," and "one of the largest" do not appear. The retreat rate is presented as "40 to 74 metres per year" — a range — not as a single dramatic figure. The lake volume is presented with its uncertainty (61.7 ± 3.7 million m³) in the accessibility text and fallback. The population figure is correctly attributed to HI-WISE 2023 and correctly uses "1.6 billion in downstream river basins" rather than the inflated "2 billion" figure. This is science communication at the level the Director Brief requires.

5. **The reduced-motion fallback (200 words) tells the complete story.** It covers: the system scale (3,500 km, 54,000 glaciers, 1.6 billion people), the grey-glacier correction, the lake's colour and physics, the 1962-to-2020 growth, the retreat rate range, the moraine dam composition and buried ice subsidence, the 2016 lake-level reduction, and the early warning system population. It includes three primary DOIs. This is a complete standalone narrative. It does NOT simply list captions — it builds an argument in the same sequence as the animation. This is correct practice for a meaningful reduced-motion experience.

6. **The aria-label coverage is strong for WCAG 2.1 AA.** Every shot has a dedicated aria-label that describes what is visible, not what the viewer should feel. Shot 09's label specifically names the colour ("milky turquoise"), the physics explanation ("glacial flour, fine particles of crushed bedrock"), the lake elevation, and the spatial relationship of calving face and moraine dam. Shot 12's label describes what happens ("a block of ice — roughly two cubic metres — shears along a debris band and pivots into the lake"), the wave size, and crucially notes "the camera does not move" — which is itself a meaningful fact about the aesthetic approach. This last detail is unusually good accessibility writing: the absence of camera movement is part of the meaning.

7. **The "silence discipline" section correctly reflects the Director Brief.** The six text elements (title, three regional labels, lake name, data pair) are fully accounted for and their absence from specific shots is explicitly justified. The justification for Shot 12's text-free status is particularly strong: "A text element — any text element — would frame this event as a moment. It is not a moment. It is Tuesday."

8. **The 61.7 million m³ figure being absent from on-screen text and present only in accessibility and fallback is the correct editorial decision.** The Director Brief is explicit: the dam's visual banality must do the work that numbers would short-circuit. Having the number available to screen-reader users and reduced-motion users without placing it in the primary visual track is the right balance.

9. **One minor scientific imprecision in the static fallback:** "The glacier retreated — from roughly 40 metres per year in the second half of the 20th century to over 70 metres per year in the early 2000s." This is correct for Imja specifically but the static fallback is presenting this as the general HKH picture (it follows the HKH-wide system description). A reader could interpret this as a general HKH retreat rate rather than Imja-specific. This is a non-blocking note: the sentence is accurate for Imja, but its placement in the paragraph (after the lake area growth figures, which are Imja-specific) means a careful reader will understand it is Imja-specific. No change required unless the production team judges the ambiguity worth addressing.

## Role 5: Blender / Animation Expert

```
ROLE: Blender / Animation Expert
VERDICT: CONDITIONAL PASS
SCORE: 7/10
```

**BLOCKING ISSUES:**

1. **The compositor text node implementation is specified but the HOW is incompletely documented.** Section 4 states: "Use the Blender compositor `Text` node (added in Blender 4.0) or pre-render text elements as PNG sequences with alpha." The Blender 4.0/5.x compositor `Text` node outputs a rendered text image in the compositor; however, the bpy Python API to CREATE and drive a compositor Text node is not specified anywhere in the technical spec. Creating a compositor Text node via bpy requires: (1) `scene.use_nodes = True`, (2) accessing `scene.node_tree`, (3) adding a `CompositorNodeText` node, (4) setting its text, font, size, and position via the node's properties, and (5) animating its opacity via a Mix node's `inputs[0].default_value` with fcurve keyframes. None of these steps are spelled out. The script author writing `ch0_cinematic.py` cannot implement compositor text from the spec as written without either prior Blender compositor Python experience or additional research. This is a documentation gap that will cause implementation ambiguity. Fix: add a code skeleton for `_create_compositor_text_overlay()` showing at minimum: how to add a CompositorNodeText, how to set font size and position in compositor space, and how to keyframe opacity via a Mix node. The "or pre-render text elements as PNG sequences" fallback is acceptable but needs its own implementation note: what tool renders the PNG sequences, what naming convention, and how does the compositor Image node reference a frame-numbered PNG sequence.

2. **The Imja_IceCliff object is listed in the scene graph (Collection: Glaciers) with visibility starting at f586, but Shots 09–11 depend on the ice cliff being visible from f586 onward.** Shot 09's description includes "At the east end: the calving ice cliff — 10–15 m above waterline, grey-brown at base (debris bands), transitioning to blue-white at fresh ice exposures." The fcurve table confirms `Imja_IceCliff hide_render: f586 = False`. This is correct for Shot 09. However, the Calving_Block — which is a separate mesh object representing the detached block — has `hide_render: f800 = False`, meaning it is invisible from f586–f799. In the 2.0 seconds of Shot 11 (f721–f780), the calving face is the primary visual subject. The Imja_IceCliff mesh at f721–f780 must show a continuous unbroken cliff face — no pre-calving gap or separation visible. The spec instructs the TD to "not animate ice cliff cracking or movement in this shot" but does not specify how to handle the geometric continuity between Imja_IceCliff (the permanent cliff) and Calving_Block (the section that separates at f800). If Calving_Block is a separate carved-out piece of Imja_IceCliff geometry, the cliff will show a gap at its eventual calving point from f586 onward (since Calving_Block is hidden but its volume is absent from the parent mesh). The spec must state: either (a) Calving_Block overlaps with Imja_IceCliff geometry until f800 (the hidden block fills the gap), or (b) Imja_IceCliff has a separate pre-calving mesh that covers the Calving_Block volume and switches off at f800 when Calving_Block appears. This is a blocking scene graph ambiguity.

3. **The Mantaflow calving cache is specified as "pre-baked; cache committed to `data/water-cycle/calving/`" but the cache commit instruction is absent from the data pipeline section (Section 6).** Section 10 (Determinism Plan) correctly identifies that the calving cache must be baked once and read-only during render, but Section 6 (Data Pipeline) does not include the calving cache in the geographic data flow diagram and does not specify the cache file format (Mantaflow `.bphys` files or FLIP Fluids cache format), the expected file count and size, or whether the cache is referenced via a relative path from the .blend file or an absolute path from the script. The script author writing `ch0_cinematic.py` needs to know: (1) what path to set for `scene.rigidbody_world.point_cache.use_disk_cache` and related properties, (2) what to check if the cache is absent (error vs. graceful rebuild), and (3) whether the Mantaflow domain settings need to be recreated in the script or whether they are hardcoded in the .blend file. Fix: add a "Calving Simulation Cache" subsection to Section 6 specifying the cache path convention, the bpy API calls to reference the existing cache, and the fallback behavior if cache is absent.

4. **The 1962 pond overlay specification states "NOT real polygon data — they are artistic approximations" and lists `IMJA_1962_PONDS` as an empty list (`[]`).** The data pipeline references `data/water-cycle/lakes/imja-1962-ponds.geojson` as a compositor overlay PNG source, but no tool or process is specified to generate that PNG from the empty polygon list. The section 6 code block shows `IMJA_1962_PONDS = []` with a comment that the ponds "render only in the compositor as amber vector outlines, not as 3D geometry." The compositor section (Section 4) describes the 1962 ponds as "Pre-rendered SVG/PNG of the 1962 pond outlines in #F5E090 (amber). The 1962 ponds are 6–8 irregular polygons, each 100–200 m diameter." But who draws these polygons, in what tool, to what file? The handoff between "artistic approximation of 6–8 irregular polygons" and "pre-rendered PNG at 1280×720" is not specified. The script author cannot implement this from the spec. Fix: either provide the actual polygon vertices for the 6–8 ponds (the spec already has approximate coordinates in `IMJA_TSHO_2020_RING` as a model), or provide a tool call (e.g., a Python script using Pillow/Cairo to draw the polygons to a 1280×720 PNG) or a design file reference.

**NON-BLOCKING NOTES:**

1. **The scene graph is substantially complete.** Seven collections covering terrain, glaciers, lake system, data overlays, sky/lighting, and camera rigs are defined. All objects required by the shot list are present: HKH DEM, sub-tile DEMs for regional shots, Khumbu_Icefall, Yala_Glacier, ImjaTsho_2020, Imja_IceCliff, Imja_Glacier_Tongue, Calving_Block, Calving_Splash, MoraineDam_Detail, SubsidenceHollow, PrayerFlag_String. The visibility fcurve table is complete and covers all objects with per-shot hide_render control.

2. **The decision to move text overlays from 3D space to compositor nodes is correct AND the intent is well-documented, even if the implementation detail is thin (see Blocking Issue 1).** The reason is clearly stated: "the script document specifies compositor-layer text with precise opacity/timing constraints that 3D text cannot satisfy." This is architecturally sound — 3D Font objects in Blender cannot be driven by compositor Mix node fcurves, cannot be precisely positioned in pixel space, and cannot participate in the compositor's color correction pass. The compositor approach is the right choice.

3. **The MAT_LakeWater Imja Tsho water material is technically achievable in Blender Cycles with the specified settings.** The combination of a Principled Volume shader with density 0.8, anisotropy 0.3 (forward Tyndall scatter), and absorption removing red/orange wavelengths will produce a milky blue-green color in the rendered output. The depth-blend between #78C8C0 (shallow) and #4AACAA (deeper) is correctly implemented as a Z-position MixRGB driver. The color verification procedure (test render Shot 09 center pixel, check ±5 units from #78C8C0 in sRGB, then adjust Volume Density and Absorption Color) is a practical and implementable QA step. The alpha=0.85 "never fully transparent" constraint is correctly enforced. The performance fallback (surface-only depth blend) is adequate. This material is achievable as specified.

4. **The geometry nodes instancing solution for 65k glacier points is adequately specified.** The three-tier strategy (Geometry Nodes instancing → particle system → capped preview) with the build_glacier_instances() function skeleton is sufficient for a developer to implement without ambiguity. The Risk Register entry for OOM (marked High likelihood) correctly identifies the failure mode and provides the mitigation chain. This is good engineering specification practice.

5. **The Calving_Block hand-keyframed animation (avoiding rigid body simulation for determinism) is the correct technical decision.** Rigid body simulations in Blender are not Cycles-seed-deterministic. Keyframing the pivot rotation (0→-45° over 9 frames) and the drop to lake surface (z: 5.025→5.010 over 12 frames) with BEZIER handles gives deterministic output and adequate physical plausibility for a 2.4 m³ block falling 1.5 m. The spec's acknowledgment that "the director constraint (pedestrian, quiet) actually reduces the visual demands" for this animation is correct.

6. **The encoding pipeline is complete and correct for the target file sizes.** The three-encoder approach (libsvtav1 CRF 32 for primary AV1, libx264 CRF 21 for H.264 fallback, libvpx-vp9 CRF 36 for scrub) with estimated sizes (4.5–6.75 MB, 8–12 MB, 2.5–4 MB respectively) is achievable within the hard caps (8 MiB, 16 MiB, 6 MiB). The VP9 fallback if libsvtav1 is absent is correctly specified. The `-g 15` keyframe interval for the scrub master is appropriate for the 0.5s seek precision needed in a UI scrubber. The `-movflags +faststart` on the H.264 output is correctly included for progressive web delivery.

7. **The poster frame is correctly specified as frame 855 (the first frame of Shot 14, the moraine dam final hold), NOT the fade-to-black end.** The spec states: "poster.jpg (frame 855 — Shot 14 moraine dam, the chapter's last non-black frame)." Shot 14 runs f856–f900 (1-indexed in the shot list) or f855–f899 (0-indexed in Blender). The spec correctly acknowledges the 0/1 indexing ambiguity: "Frame 855 = 0-indexed frame 855 in the sequence... OR if Blender outputs 1-indexed: '0856.exr' — verify against actual output naming." This is the right poster frame: it shows the milky turquoise lake above the moraine dam, which is the share card the Director Brief specifies as the chapter's "OG image." The POSTER_FRAME=855 variable in the encoding script is correct for the 0-indexed convention confirmed in the spec's Overview (Section 1: "frame indices 0–899 inclusive in Blender; note the old script used 0-indexed, this spec continues that convention").

8. **Render time estimates are plausible but optimistic.** The production preset estimate is "3–5 hrs" at 128 spp + OIDN for 900 frames at 1280×720 with OPTIX. Given the scene complexity — five geographic regions, volumetric lake water, the Mantaflow composite, atmospheric volume for Shots 01–03, and DOF on three shots — the lower end of this estimate (3 hrs) is likely for a high-end NVIDIA GPU (RTX 4090 or equivalent). For mid-range hardware (RTX 3070), production render time is more plausibly 6–8 hrs. The `production_atmosphere` preset at 256 spp, estimated at 6–10 hrs, is realistic. These estimates should be treated as aspirational targets, not hard commitments, particularly for the 390 frames of volumetric lake shots (Shots 09–14). This is not a blocking issue — the estimates are directionally correct and the risk register notes the tradeoff.

9. **One missing implementation detail that would help a developer:** the script calls `seed_mod.lock_seeds(Path(__file__))` but the technical spec never specifies the signature of `lock_seeds()` (does it return a hash, set Blender's Cycles seed as a side effect, or both?). Section 10 shows `bpy.context.scene.cycles.seed = int(sha256[:8], 16) & 0x7FFF_FFFF` as the derivation, but whether this is what `lock_seeds()` does internally or whether the script must do this explicitly is unclear. For strict reproducibility, the spec should either show the full `lock_seeds()` implementation or confirm it is inherited from the existing `shared/seed_mod.py` without modification.

10. **The `SubsidenceHollow` object is described as an Empty (locator) in the scene graph but as a boolean modifier target in the material section.** Section 1 lists it as "Empty | Locator for the subsidence hollow position. Used to position MAT_MoraineDam subsidence in procedural noise." Section 3 (MAT_MoraineDam) describes it as: "A separate mesh object (SubsidenceHollow) driven by a boolean modifier applied to MoraineDam_Detail creates a ~5–8 m diameter, ~0.5 m deep bowl." These two descriptions are inconsistent: an Empty cannot serve as a boolean modifier target (that requires a Mesh). This is a minor type conflict in the scene graph specification. Fix: change the scene graph entry for SubsidenceHollow from "Empty" to "Mesh" (a shallow disc or bowl geometry), or change the material section approach to use the Empty as a procedural noise anchor (position-driven Noise Texture mask) rather than a boolean modifier. Either approach is valid; the spec currently specifies both simultaneously.
